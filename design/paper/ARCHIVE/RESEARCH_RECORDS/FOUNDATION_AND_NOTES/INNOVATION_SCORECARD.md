## Justification for the central 4/5 scores

- **Novelty 4:** the reviewed close work covers data consistency, posterior uncertainty, interpolation ambiguity, and spacing difficulty, but not a same-target, controlled measurement-overlap experiment for deterministic consensus blindness. The audit is bounded; no claim of exhaustive global novelty is made.
- **Importance 4:** 81–87% shared error makes ordinary agreement structurally incomplete. The experiment identifies a design variable that governs whether that dominant failure becomes visible.
- **Generality 4:** two independent MRI cohorts, HAT and SwinIR, and linear/cubic analytical interpolation support the phenomenon. It is still limited to through-plane MRI and requires evaluation in other inverse problems.
- **Mechanistic depth 4:** overlap is manipulated while target, model, case, plane, and foreground mask are paired; zero, natural one-anchor, and maximal overlap controls distinguish shared information from raw diversity.
- **Predictive power 4:** the hypothesis predicted the sign of the zero-vs-maximal effect before the decisive experiment and separately predicted x4→x2 benefit ranking; overbroad predictions were rejected.
- **Method consequence 4:** the result yields a falsifiable intervention-design principle and a concrete phase-grid stress-test protocol. It is not falsely sold as an always-superior uncertainty score.
- **Experimental rigor 4:** exact endpoint equality, case-cluster bootstrap, OOF independent cohort, per-architecture replication, analytical baselines, raw-magnitude control, common mask, and negative deployment tests are included.
- **Reviewer resilience 4:** obvious attacks—larger perturbation, Transformer-specific bias, target mismatch, mask mismatch, repeated-measurement pseudoreplication, and universal-method overclaim—are tested or explicitly bounded.

The remaining gap to 5/5 is external validation outside MRI and prospective acquisition data rather than dense-volume simulation.
