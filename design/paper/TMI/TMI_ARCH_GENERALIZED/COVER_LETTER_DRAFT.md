Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider “Measurement Dependence Governs Disagreement-Based Reliability in Deterministic MRI Slice Interpolation” as a Regular Paper in IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging.

The manuscript studies a technical quality-assurance failure in deterministic through-plane MRI interpolation: inaccurate reconstructions can agree when they reuse physical acquired-slice evidence. It does not present the elementary cancellation identity as new. The contribution is a target-preserving physical measurement-overlap intervention and a three-cohort characterization of how anchor reuse changes error alignment, disagreement observability and examination-level selective risk. It includes fastMRI and IXI brain T2 examinations, an internal NingXia knee T2 cohort, two frozen architectures, seven endpoints, analytical x2/x4/x6 controls and explicit operating assumptions.

At a prespecified 70% IXI coverage, measurement-disjoint scoring retained none of 29 high-error examinations, compared with two under maximal sharing, one under an architecture committee and three under adjacent-anchor MAE. This is framed as retrospective reconstruction-QA prioritization, not diagnostic or patient-safety evidence. Unfavorable boundaries are retained: structural metrics are cohort dependent, an adjacent baseline is strongest on NingXia, and no score is universally best.
