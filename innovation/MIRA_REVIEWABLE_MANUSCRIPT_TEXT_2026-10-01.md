## Abstract

Agreement between MRI reconstructions can conceal error when the reconstructions reuse observed slices. We study this failure in retrospective through-plane T2 slice interpolation by holding the target and a frozen reference prediction fixed while changing the real-slice context supplied to a companion estimator. Our measurement-dependence audit measures the fraction of paired-error energy visible in prediction disagreement; it does not treat that retrospective fraction as a deployable uncertainty score. In previously studied fastMRI and IXI cohorts, disjoint companion contexts increased this fraction across the frozen cross-architecture comparison matrix. Acquired-only finite-response analyses further associated correctly matched physical anchors with error alignment beyond exact context categories in HAT and SwinIR, although these exploratory analyses do not isolate anchor identity causally. In a separately sourced RRMS T2 cohort, one prespecified HAT-to-SwinIR comparison reproduced the observability gap across 84 examinations and 479 targets: the patient-weighted disjoint-minus-shared difference was 19.76 percentage points (95% patient-bootstrap interval, 19.33–20.16). Exploratory controls retained the direction, but a later 60-patient heldout test found no prediction gain from continuous response influence beyond an exact-context baseline. These results support a bounded audit of shared measurement dependence, not a calibrated per-case error detector or a claim of true thin-slice recovery.

## External Cohort Methods

We evaluated the public MSValid RRMS brain T2 cohort (Zenodo record 10875606) as a separately sourced retrospective confirmation. Before reading target planes, we froze the first available T2 volume per patient, a geometry-qualified set of 84 examinations and 479 residue-1/3 targets, the fastMRI-trained HAT and SwinIR checkpoints, CPU float32 inference, an acquired-only foreground support, and patient-level inference. HAT used its phase-0 real-slice context throughout. SwinIR was evaluated with the same phase-0 anchors and with a phase-2 context that shared no physical anchor with HAT. Both companion contexts reconstructed the same withheld native plane; the native scans were retrospectively subsampled x4, so this experiment did not measure recovery of independently acquired thin slices. The primary endpoint was the patient-weighted difference in the fraction of paired-error energy visible in output disagreement, with the two target residues weighted equally within patient. The prespecified interval used 5,000 paired patient bootstrap draws. Source-file, checkpoint and script hashes, inclusion geometry and the original wrapper-equivalence checks are retained in the reproducibility archive.

## External Cohort Results

All 84 examinations and 479 qualified targets were scored. Patient-weighted observability was 2.84% with shared anchors and 22.60% with disjoint anchors, a prespecified increase of 19.76 percentage points (95% patient-bootstrap interval, 19.33–20.16); the patient-level difference was positive in all 84 examinations. Mean SwinIR error did not increase in the disjoint condition (patient-weighted MAE 139.06 versus 141.68 in within-volume raw intensity units). Exploratory phase reversal, a linear companion, and shared six-anchor min/max normalization produced differences of 19.50, 14.62, and 19.48 percentage points, respectively. A post-result algebraic decomposition attributed only 0.15 percentage points of the main observability change to companion MSE imbalance in the endpoint formula; the remaining 19.61 points accompanied a drop in true paired-error cosine from 0.944 to 0.550. This decomposition is descriptive and uses the same error quantities as the endpoint, not independent proof of a physical causal pathway.

## Response Mechanism And Failure Boundary

In the original fastMRI and IXI response archives, correctly matched physical-anchor responses retained a positive association with true paired-error cosine after adjustment for both target and exact context. HAT and SwinIR same-architecture controls and HAT-to-SwinIR cross-architecture controls all exceeded the mean of two deliberately mismatched response pairings in a post-result six-contrast simultaneous sensitivity analysis. The weakest simultaneous lower limit, for fastMRI HAT-to-SwinIR, was only 0.0009. SAINT showed positive exact-context-adjusted association but not consistent superiority over each mismatched pairing, so three-architecture anchor-identity specificity is not claimed. More importantly, in the new MSValid source, a model fitted on the first 24 patients did not improve paired-error-cosine prediction on the remaining 60 patients when continuous influence was added to an exact-context and slice-spacing baseline (patient-weighted MAE 0.027448 versus 0.027280; exploratory baseline-minus-augmented interval crossed zero). Mean within-target candidate ranking was unchanged at 0.935. The response experiments therefore inform a conditional mechanism audit; they do not yield a reliable patient-level correctness or context-selection score.

## Limitations

The shared-to-disjoint comparison changes companion slice content, signed geometry and acquisition phase along with physical overlap. Post-result normalization and phase controls reduce simple alternative explanations but do not identify a pure anchor-ID causal effect. The MSValid confirmation covers one ordered HAT-to-SwinIR pair, not the original 20-pair architecture matrix. The native T2 data are thick-slice examinations retrospectively subsampled x4; no independently acquired thin-slice ground truth, lesion-level annotation or clinical outcome was assessed. The old cohorts were repeatedly used during method development, so their exploratory confidence intervals do not account for the full history of selection. The remaining-patient influence test shares the MSValid source and was designed after earlier responses were inspected; it is not an independent external replication. Historical failure of the acquired-only actionability guard and the absence of a calibrated correctness score remain explicit. Dataset release, image-use and local-cohort ethics documentation must be resolved before a submission version can include the corresponding material.
