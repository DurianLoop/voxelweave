# When Shared Slice Context Hides Paired Reconstruction Error: A Three-Source Audit of Through-Plane MRI Interpolation

**Internal scientific draft, 2026-10-01. ## Abstract

Agreement between two MRI slice interpolators need not reveal their common error when both reuse the same observed slices. We quantify this dependence with a fixed-reference, same-target audit: a frozen HAT prediction is paired with a frozen SwinIR prediction made either from shared or disjoint released-slice contexts. We retrospectively evaluate the fraction of paired error visible in output disagreement using one IXI-T2-trained model pair and observed-anchor foreground support. A same-patient, result-informed follow-up on 84 MSValid RRMS examinations (479 targets) yielded a patient-weighted disjoint-minus-shared difference of 24.19 percentage points (95% patient-bootstrap interval 23.71–24.67). A later protocol-frozen ReMIND test scored 62/63 brain-tumor patients (248 targets), yielding 26.40 points (95% stratified patient-bootstrap interval 25.69–27.17). A further, later-informed TCIA UPENN-GBM processed-DICOM check scored all 374 selected patients (1,496 targets), yielding 28.12 points (95% patient-bootstrap interval 27.90–28.33); all selected UPENN series were derived/secondary, and paired MSE did not improve. Post-result common-normalization, phase-reversal and linear-partner controls on the first two sources retained the direction; a matched-geometry synthetic-noise contrast was much smaller. A further post-result analytic PCHIP/linear pair produced even larger contrasts on the same MSValid/ReMIND targets (29.25/30.96 points), ruling out a learned-architecture-specific interpretation. The fixed-pair results document dependence of retrospective paired-error visibility on released context. They do not isolate original acquisition identity, produce a deployable error score, demonstrate pathology preservation, or establish true thin-slice recovery.

## 1. Introduction

Comparing two reconstruction outputs is a tempting way to assess reliability without a ground-truth target. Yet two models may agree because they process the same evidence and inherit aligned errors. This possibility is established in ensemble theory and MRI error work [1–3]. Its practical size under a controlled change of observed slice context is less clear for through-plane interpolation.

We study a deliberately limited question. For one frozen cross-architecture pair and one withheld published target plane, does the fraction of paired error visible in model disagreement change when only the companion model switches to a disjoint set of published context slices? The fixed reference and target prevent a change of either from explaining the contrast. The intervention still changes the companion's complete neighboring images, signed geometry and potentially preprocessing, so the resulting estimate is a *context contrast*, not an identity-only causal effect.

Our contribution is an auditable fixed-pair protocol and its source-specific evaluation on three public patient strata with distinct release histories. The analysis keeps retrospective error observability separate from deployable confidence: the former requires the withheld target for evaluation. This distinction matters because supervised MRI error estimators and calibrated uncertainty intervals already address different downstream questions [1,4]; no such performance is claimed here.

## 2. Prior Work and Claim Boundary

Hu et al. [1] explicitly show that MRI prediction variability can miss systematic error and train an error-prediction model. Classical ambiguity and negative-correlation analyses [2,3] likewise establish that correlated errors affect ensemble behavior. Our distinction is the paired, fixed-reference intervention on released physical slice contexts under an explicit information contract, with full patient-level accounting in three source strata. We do not claim discovery of common error or a new energy identity.

HAT and SwinIR [5,6] are image-restoration architectures used here through frozen local 2.5D MRI training wrappers, not official MRI checkpoints. Through-plane interpolation and complementary-slice acquisition predate this study [7]. Recent supervised pixelwise MRI intervals [4] and range/null ambiguity decomposition [8] cover uncertainty and identifiability concepts that should not be relabeled as our innovation. They address different forward operators, training targets or guarantees; our endpoint remains retrospective paired-error visibility for the tested slice contexts.

Two further MRI precedents narrow the language. A measured-subspace audit of diffusion posterior samples tests whether reconstructions conditioned on one fixed multi-coil k-space measurement disagree on already acquired coefficients, then applies a consistency lock [12]. Input-perturbation/local-Lipschitz work relates output response to MRI reconstruction error and proposes an action threshold [13]. Neither measures the present same-target published-slice context contrast, but they preclude a broad claim to originate black-box measurement auditing or sensitivity-based reliability scoring. Our analysis changes a companion's published context and requires target truth; it supplies neither a k-space consistency guarantee nor a deployable threshold.

A clinical 2D brain MRI reconstruction study also estimated fully sampled PSNR and MSE from undersampled data by repeatedly simulating new sampling/noise realizations from a reconstruction and correcting with held-out validation data [14]. Deep-ensemble predictive uncertainty has been evaluated across multiple fastMRI architectures and acquisition/domain shifts [15]. NPB-REC [16] samples nine weight states for the same undersampled multi-coil k-space input and relates image-level prediction standard deviation to MSE. Earlier single-coil work by Edupuganti et al. [17] separates reconstruction variance from bias and estimates risk with SURE under explicit residual assumptions. These works rule out novelty language about MRI error estimation without target truth, input resampling, uncertainty missing bias or cross-model uncertainty in general. Reference [15] was verified at the indexed abstract level only; its full experimental protocol requires inspection before any priority claim. None of the read methods supplies the specific released through-plane slice-context estimand defined below.

Task-level MRI work further limits the interpretation. *Mind the Detail* [19] generates data-consistent, semantically diverse reconstructions from one undersampled knee k-space input and evaluates pathology recall/mAP on fastMRI+ annotations. *Evaluating Structural Uncertainty* [20] evaluates segmentation-volume variability and bias for healthy T1 reconstructions from the same 5x undersampled k-space input within seed ensembles. Neither read protocol switches a companion's released through-plane context while fixing the target/reference, but both show why pixel agreement alone cannot establish a clinical consequence. The [bounded forward-citation audit](literature/2026-10-01_four_seed_forward_citation_result.md) is not a priority certificate; Kuestner [15] and other relevant methods remain unread at full-text level.

More recent full-text neighbors further constrain the claim. *SliceBridge* [21] restores withheld/corrupted T1 intervals from bilateral intact slices and improves through-plane consistency over a same-context uncoupled-flow model on 581 external IXI subjects. Its matched downstream regional-volume error comparison was not significant (448 eligible cases; adjusted p=0.370), despite a large consistency gain. A glioma task-aware UQ study [22] evaluates dropout and ensembles against segmentation/classification labels using error detection and selective prediction. Neither performs our fixed-reference, switched-companion published-context contrast, but [21] rules out broad through-plane-context novelty and [22] rules out presenting retrospective `O` as a first task-risk indicator. See the [source-checked full-text audit](literature/2026-10-01_targeted_recent_fulltext_result.md).

An earlier neonatal T2 study [23] already replaced motion-affected slices by shape-preserving cubic interpolation and assessed downstream brain-tissue segmentation, including failures with adjacent corrupted neighbors and volume bias. Its full text further excludes a generic task-level novelty claim for T2 slice interpolation. It did not test this paper's fixed-reference paired-error context switch; a future tumor-region experiment would need a same-information cubic comparator and direct, same-grid labels. The [official UPENN NIfTI package inventory](UPENN_NIFTI_OFFICIAL_PACKAGE_INVENTORY_RESULT_2026-10-01.md) currently lists only 147 candidate corrected-label files among 232 CSV-marked scans, and no T2/mask headers have been validated.

Segmentation-guided MRI reconstruction [24] has also produced upper- and lower-volume segmentations by guiding diffusion reconstructions from the same undersampled knee k-space input, outperforming repeated sampling on its reported segmentation-bound precision and recall. This is not a released-neighbor intervention, but it precludes a generic claim that reconstruction diversity was first linked to segmentation uncertainty here. The [full-text audit](literature/2026-10-01_segmentation_guided_mri_fulltext_result.md) records its experimental contract and numerical results.

Earlier PHiRec work [25] generated 20 reconstructions per fixed SKM-TEA knee k-space input and propagated them through a segmentation U-Net, comparing both reconstruction variance and segmentation gamma maps with error maps under acceleration shifts. It likewise did not switch released through-plane neighbors, but already tested reconstruction uncertainty against downstream segmentation error. Our `Delta O` cannot be described as the first such task-aware MRI analysis, nor as a substitute for its label-based endpoint; see the [preprint full-text audit](literature/2026-10-01_fischer_mri_uq_fulltext_result.md).

## 3. Data and Frozen Inference

**Training source and models.** Both checkpoints are local HAT/SwinIR 2.5D implementations trained on public IXI T2 [9] and fixed before these analyses. The stored checkpoint SHA-256 values and inference script hashes appear in the [MSValid result](MSVALID_IXI_WEIGHTS_SECONDARY_RESULT_2026-10-01.md) and [ReMIND protocol](REMIND_NATIVE_T2_CONFIRMATION_PROTOCOL_2026-10-01.md). Inference uses CPU float32 and two PyTorch threads; no new training is included in this paper.

**MSValid.** The public Zenodo collection [10] contributes the first available T2 volume for each of 84 geometry-qualified RRMS patients. There are 479 preselected residue-1/3 published target planes. Native through-plane spacing is 3–6 mm. This analysis reuses patients already evaluated with another weight pair; the IXI weight-pair comparison was fixed only after that earlier result had been inspected. It is therefore a same-patient secondary result, not an independent source confirmation of the original discovery.

**ReMIND.** Official TCIA/NBIA downloads [11] supply all 63 available preoperative `2D_AX_T2` (36 patients) and `2D_AX_T2_BLADE` (27 patients) series in the frozen inventory, without patient overlap between these two protocol groups. Header-only checks excluded one ordinary-T2 series with 21 layers under the predeclared 24-layer threshold, leaving 35 ordinary and 27 BLADE patients. Each contributed two targets in each residue class, 248 targets overall. The median released z spacing is about 5.0 mm in ordinary T2 and 2.5 mm in BLADE. DICOM `ImageType` includes `MPR` across both groups; neither series label nor layer geometry proves that published slices are independently acquired raw planes. The protocol was written after the MSValid result but before any ReMIND target pixels were decoded.

**Target and context contract.** Physical layer locations, not filenames or DICOM InstanceNumber, determine ReMIND z order. With zero-based ordered planes, phase 0 contains `0,4,8,...` and phase 2 contains `2,6,10,...`. Targets have residue 1 or 3 and sufficient real planes on both sides. HAT phase 0 supplies fixed prediction `a`. SwinIR phase 0 supplies shared partner `b_s`; SwinIR phase 2 supplies disjoint partner `b_d`, predicting the same withheld target `y`. The original models normalize each input phase from all its observed anchors, resize to 256 x 256 and use a four-channel left/right/linear-coarse/relative-position condition; the output residual is returned to native in-plane size and intensity range. ReMIND DICOM rescale slope/intercept is applied per plane. Inputs never use the target pixel intensities. A post-result negative control replaces each SwinIR partner with native-intensity linear interpolation of its two real bracketing planes at the same quarter/three-quarter index offset; the HAT reference, target, support and patient aggregation stay fixed.

**Evaluation support.** The foreground support is the union of pixels exceeding 1% of each nearest phase-0 anchor's maximum absolute intensity. It is determined from observed anchors alone and held fixed between the two companion conditions. This support is not a lesion mask. The reference output `a`, target `y` and support are exactly shared across the paired comparison.

**UPENN-GBM processed T2.** The official TCIA Version 2 source [18] yielded 402 candidate series with the literal axial T2 processed description across 374 patients. A protocol written after the MSValid and ReMIND results selected one earliest study/series per patient before target pixels, then fixed four odd targets per eligible DICOM series. All 374 selected series passed nonpixel qualification and all 1,496 targets were scored. Independent ZIP/CRC and 23,702-header checks confirmed every selected series carries DERIVED/SECONDARY/OTHER image-type flags; 367 have a physical z step of 3.0 mm and seven have 4.8 mm. The publisher's atlas-registered NIfTI labels explicitly do not align to DICOM by design; no pathology label was used [18]. This is a processed released-plane test, not a native-acquisition or clinical validation.

## 4. Estimand and Statistical Unit

For errors `e_a=a-y` and `e_b=b-y` on the fixed support, define

$$
T=\frac{\operatorname{MSE}(a,y)+\operatorname{MSE}(b,y)}{2},\qquad
O=\frac{\operatorname{mean}[(a-b)^2]}{4T}.
$$

`O` is the fraction of paired error energy visible in disagreement, with retrospective access to `y` only for evaluation. The principal source-specific contrast is `Delta O = O(a,b_d,y)-O(a,b_s,y)`. For each patient, targets are first averaged within residue 1/3, then the two residues are equally weighted; patients are the independent units. MSValid reports its frozen 5,000-draw patient bootstrap interval. ReMIND uses its frozen 5,000-draw bootstrap stratified by the two 2D protocol groups, weighting strata by completed patient counts. We report group estimates without treating targets or the two protocol groups as additional independent confirmations.

An exact algebraic identity expresses `O` through paired-error cosine and the two MSE terms. This identity is a diagnostic, not a new theorem or independent experiment. The output difference alone does not measure total error and cannot be used as a calibrated patient-level error score from the present data.

UPENN followed the same fixed-model phase-0/phase-2 target rule and patient-equal residue aggregation. Its protocol specified 5,000 patient-bootstrap draws and a primary success rule of at least 300 complete patients with a positive lower bound; both passed. The original MSValid/ReMIND results were already known at protocol creation, so neither the UPENN interval nor the third source count corrects research-selection uncertainty.

UPENN's 374/374 patients and 1,496/1,496 targets were positive. Its patient-equal paired MSE was 29,510.46 shared versus 29,601.78 disjoint in native DICOM intensity units, so improved observability is **not** a paired-reconstruction-quality gain. Every selected series was derived/secondary, and a [source-specific label check](UPENN_GBM_LABEL_ALIGNMENT_GATE_RESULT_2026-10-01.md) found the publisher explicitly warns that its tumor-labelled atlas NIfTI does not align with DICOM by design. These facts rule out native acquired-plane and lesion-level interpretations of this cohort.

ReMIND's ordinary-T2 group had a +26.5156-point mean (35/35 positive); BLADE had +26.2500 points (27/27 positive). Both groups met their prespecified count and direction thresholds; the pooled interval lower bound exceeded zero. The only failure was the documented pre-pixel short series. Result-after common min/max controls used the union of real anchors for both phase normalizations and yielded +24.0358 points on [MSValid](MSVALID_IXI_WEIGHT_POSTRESULT_CONTROLS_RESULT_2026-10-01.md) and +26.3236 on [ReMIND](REMIND_POSTRESULT_CONTROLS_RESULT_2026-10-01.md). Fixing HAT phase 2 and using a phase-2 acquired-only support yielded +24.0681 and +26.3049 points, respectively. All 84 MSValid and 62 ReMIND patient differences remained positive in both exploratory directions. These checks rule out independent min/max and one chosen reference phase as sole explanations, but not neighboring image content or geometry.

The [post-result native-linear partner control](PUBLIC_LINEAR_PARTNER_CONTROL_RESULT_2026-10-01.md) reused every original target, fixed HAT prediction and support. Its shared/disjoint `O` was 5.8641%/26.4085% on MSValid and 5.6629%/28.6015% on ReMIND; patient-weighted `Delta O` remained +20.5445 points (95% patient-bootstrap interval +20.1283 to +20.9729; 84/84 positive) and +22.9386 points (95% stratified patient-bootstrap interval +22.3772 to +23.5257; 62/62 positive). Both ReMIND 2D groups were positive. This directly limits a learned-pair-specific reading of the main result. Because the linear and SwinIR partners have different errors and `O` uses each partner's MSE in its denominator, subtracting their contrasts does not identify a neural-network contribution.

A later [fully analytic PCHIP-reference/linear-partner falsification](TWO_SOURCE_ANALYTIC_CONTEXT_FALSIFICATION_RESULT_2026-10-01.md) held all 727 targets and their original supports fixed but used no learned predictions. Its patient-weighted shared/disjoint `O` was 0.5830%/29.8300% on MSValid and 0.6823%/31.6407% on ReMIND, yielding `Delta O` of +29.2470 points (95% exploratory patient interval +28.9278 to +29.5750; 84/84 positive) and +30.9585 points (+30.4938 to +31.4231; 62/62 positive). The original neural contrasts on the same targets were +24.1856 and +26.3999 points. This post-result control demonstrates that a large released-context contrast needs no learned architecture. Its reference and companion error magnitudes differ from the neural pair, so the numerical analytic-minus-neural gap is not an estimate of network contribution or reconstruction quality. Independent endpoint verification covered all target/patient formulae, four intervals and one pixel replay per source.

The [post-result matched-geometry synthetic-noise control](MSVALID_MATCHED_GEOMETRY_NOISE_CONTROL_RESULT_2026-10-01.md) kept both networks on the same phase-0 anatomical planes, locations and clean-anchor normalization range, changing only whether SwinIR shared HAT's generated magnitude-noise realization. On all 84 MSValid patients and 479 targets per level, independent-minus-shared `O` was -0.000339 percentage points at 1% noise (95% patient-bootstrap interval -0.001348 to +0.000604; 41/84 positive) and +0.002529 points at 5% noise (+0.000276 to +0.004900; 46/84 positive). Both magnitudes are small compared with the original +24.1856-point phase contrast. The 5% interval excludes zero, but this is an arbitrary synthetic perturbation on already published magnitude planes, not a repeated acquisition; its result cannot apportion the original contrast among noise identity, anatomy and geometry.

In a later [post-result equal-information comparison](TWO_SOURCE_EQUAL_INFORMATION_CONTROLS_RESULT_2026-10-01.md), the equal-weight HAT0/SwinIR2 average lowered MSE relative to the shared-context average for all 727 original targets. Giving standard interpolation access to the same union of phase-0 and phase-2 published planes narrowed that practical consequence. Relative to a four-plane PCHIP interpolator, the learned average had a patient-mean MSE advantage of 8.12% on MSValid (95% exploratory patient interval 6.94% to 9.33%; 84/84 patients) but only 0.91% on ReMIND (interval -0.52% to 2.42%; 34/62 patients). Linear and Catmull-Rom results and all patient rows are reported in the linked control. These comparisons do not establish a robust new ensembling method: they were designed after the main effect, and phase-2 planes are unavailable under the original phase-0-only x4 input.

In a [post-result fixed-denominator sensitivity](FIXED_DENOMINATOR_TWO_SOURCE_RESULT_2026-10-01.md), both conditions for each target used the shared-condition paired MSE in the denominator. Patient-weighted disjoint-minus-shared disagreement remained +24.2152 points on MSValid (95% patient-bootstrap interval +23.6886 to +24.7320) and +26.4918 points on ReMIND (+25.7382 to +27.2917). All 479 and 248 original targets, respectively, increased in raw squared output disagreement. The same [post-result UPENN calculation](UPENN_FIXED_DENOMINATOR_RESULT_2026-10-01.md) yielded +28.4961 points (+28.2633 to +28.7296; 374/374 patients and 1,496/1,496 targets positive). This excludes a changing paired-MSE denominator as the sole explanation in all three strata, but recomputes the same predictions and cannot distinguish anatomy, geometry or acquisition identity.

In a result-after [algebraic decomposition](REMIND_ERROR_ALIGNMENT_DECOMPOSITION_RESULT_2026-10-01.md), companion MSE imbalance contributed +0.0779 points and the error-alignment component +26.3220 points to the original ReMIND `Delta O`; the terms close the observed effect by identity, so this is explanation of the measured endpoint rather than new evidence.

## 6. Discussion and Limitations

The later UPENN test extends the phenomenon to a distinct processed release, but its 374 positive patients cannot repair the missing original-acquisition counterfactual or supply a lesion outcome. Its paired MSE was slightly worse under disjoint context; the reported change concerns error visibility, not reconstruction quality. The publisher's segmentation labels are aligned to atlas NIfTI, explicitly not to these DICOMs by design. A clinical task test needs a new verified alignment or a separate NIfTI-space protocol.

Across the tested pair, a small shared-context disagreement coexists with substantial paired error, while a disjoint published-slice context exposes more of that error. The fixed target and HAT output make this more specific than an unpaired comparison of two reconstruction methods. A simple linear partner preserves much of the contrast; shared-input dependence is therefore not unique to two learned architectures. Under matched published anatomy and geometry, changing only simulated noise identity did not reproduce the large phase contrast. This does not show that actual acquisition identity is irrelevant: the simulation uses arbitrary noise levels, a magnitude-noise approximation and clean-anchor normalization, and cannot mimic motion or the acquisition/reconstruction chain. The original intervention still changes complete neighboring planes and signed relative positions. The per-phase input range may also change; common-scale sensitivity controls only that range difference and leaks two extrema from the other phase into the shared condition. ReMIND's MPR DICOM flag further prevents an inference about independent original acquisition events. No actual thick-to-thin acquisition, lesion preservation, diagnostic decision or clinical outcome was tested.

The fully analytic MSValid/ReMIND control, together with the separate MSD tumor/local control, shows that ordinary interpolation can reproduce both large global and small region-specific released-context effects. This weakens novelty claims based on model uncertainty or architecture-specific shared errors. The remaining contribution is a transparent, source-bounded measurement contract rather than a new reconstruction or uncertainty method; its publication value still depends on direct prior-art exclusion and an independently meaningful endpoint.

Mean error visibility is distinct from identifying a higher-error target. In a [post-result within-patient, within-residue ranking check](TWO_SOURCE_TARGET_ERROR_RANKING_RESULT_2026-10-01.md), HAT/SwinIR output-disagreement concordance increased from 0.661 to 0.802 on MSValid but only from 0.613 to 0.677 on ReMIND; the latter's paired patient interval for the improvement crossed zero. Disjoint SwinIR exceeded a disjoint native-linear score in both sources, but the phase-2 images required by both disjoint scores are unavailable under the intended phase-0-only x4 input. These findings do not establish a deployable or calibrated error score.

Likewise, the same-information PCHIP comparison has no clear ReMIND ensemble advantage. A positive retrospective error-observability contrast and even a lower ensemble MSE on the original targets therefore cannot be promoted to a general reconstruction-performance claim without a prospectively evaluated, equally informed x2 model and a fresh patient source.

The public-source route deliberately drops the older fastMRI/NingXia matrix. It therefore does not claim broad architecture replication, a new interpolator or calibrated case-level risk prediction. The MSValid IXI-weight result reused patients after an earlier result influenced model-pair selection; ReMIND was chosen with that history known, and UPENN was designed after both positive results. Patient bootstrap intervals describe sampling within these fixed cohorts, not research-selection uncertainty. Anonymous identifiers cannot prove absolute cross-collection patient non-overlap. Raw intensity MAE is meaningful only within paired scans and does not compare disease groups in physical units.

This audit's practical message is limited but direct: agreement between frozen interpolators that reuse the same published slices should not be treated as independent evidence of correctness, whether the partner is another network or simple interpolation. A future study that seeks a stronger acquisition-identity claim needs sequence provenance establishing which planes are independently acquired, matched geometry/content controls, and a prospectively isolated target. A deployable detector additionally requires an acquired-only score and external ranking/calibration tests against supervised error methods [1,4].
