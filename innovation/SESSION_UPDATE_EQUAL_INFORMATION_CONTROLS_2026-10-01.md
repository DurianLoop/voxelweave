The persistent user goal is to keep researching until a defensible publishable MRI claim is reached, without disturbing the terminal's ongoing GPU training. The previous QIN full-cohort header turn made progress by ruling out one exact-plane acquisition-identity source. This turn tested whether the current MIRA context-audit effect also yields a useful reconstruction improvement when the **same extra published observations** are available to simple interpolation. No process control, GPU call, IXI locked test image or training code was touched.

Three sequentially frozen, post-main-result calculations used the same IXI-trained HAT/SwinIR pair and original 727 targets. First, equal-weight prediction averaging reduced MSE on all 727 targets when the partner changed from shared to disjoint context. Next, with phase-0 and phase-2 native planes equally available, this average beat nearest-plane linear interpolation by a patient mean 7.61% on MSValid and 2.41% on ReMIND. Finally, the fixed four-plane Catmull-Rom and PCHIP tests found that ReMIND's ensemble advantage over PCHIP shrank to **0.91%**, 95% exploratory patient interval **[-0.52%, +2.42%]**, with only **34/62** patients favoring the ensemble. All planned baselines, full counts, source hashes and statistical limits are in the [result](TWO_SOURCE_EQUAL_INFORMATION_CONTROLS_RESULT_2026-10-01.md).
