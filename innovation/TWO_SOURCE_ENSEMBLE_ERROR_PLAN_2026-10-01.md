# Frozen calculation: error of shared vs disjoint partner averages

**Status:** post-main-result exploratory calculation, written before computing this endpoint. The positive `Delta O` and source histories are already known. This test is a consequence check, not independent confirmation or a prospective success claim. It reads only the frozen per-target CSVs; no image, checkpoint or GPU operation is needed.

Use the original IXI-trained HAT/SwinIR records for every 84 MSValid patients/479 targets and 62 ReMIND patients/248 targets, without exclusion or target reselection. The MSValid `per_target.csv` SHA-256 is `ad2871338d1e4288913327eb5935d9056acd70bb447640d1f288a36533830ba4`; ReMIND is `450def82f8f03261b55c36ffaed952ee865e7c3be83c0c5b77df1f6a8e441e1c`. Verify these digests and the source patient counts before calculation. Retain the same fixed HAT reference, target and phase-0 foreground support.

For each target and context `c`, calculate the equal-weight output-average MSE from the original paired MSE and observability: `E_c=MSE((a+b_c)/2,y)=T_c(1-O_c)`. Compare `E_shared-E_disjoint`; positive values favor the disjoint context. First average target MSEs within residue 1 and 3, then equally average the residue means per patient. The primary descriptive statistic is the **patient mean of individual relative reductions** `(E_shared-E_disjoint)/E_shared`, not the ratio of unpaired cross-patient intensity scales. Count improved/worsened patients and targets, retain all four individual model MSEs, and separately report source-specific absolute MSE differences for transparency. Use 5,000 paired patient bootstrap draws with seed `20261001` for MSValid. For ReMIND, stratify 35 ordinary T2 and 27 BLADE patients, resample each group at its completed size with seed `20261002`, and combine by 35/62 and 27/62. These intervals are exploratory and conditional on the already selected models and sources.

The result cannot be sold as a deployable gain under phase-0-only x4 input: the disjoint partner requires phase-2 published slices that the sparse input lacks. It also cannot identify a neural-network-specific effect, since the earlier native-linear partner has a large `Delta O`. If the disjoint ensemble fails to reduce MSE consistently across both sources, stop using `Delta O` as evidence that simply averaging the outputs helps. If it succeeds, describe only an additional-context retrospective consequence; a fair equal-information-budget or newly acquired-slice study would still be required for an actionable method claim.
