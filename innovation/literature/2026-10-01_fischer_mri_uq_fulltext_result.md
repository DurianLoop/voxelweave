# Fischer 2023 MRI reconstruction-to-segmentation uncertainty: full-text audit

Fischer, Kuestner and Baumgartner, *Uncertainty Estimation and Propagation in Accelerated MRI Reconstruction*, arXiv:2308.02631v1 (2023), proceedings DOI `10.1007/978-3-031-44336-7_9`. The public [arXiv HTML](source_cache/fischer_mri_uq_2026-10-01/article.html), [extracted text](source_cache/fischer_mri_uq_2026-10-01/article.txt) and [retrieval hash](source_cache/fischer_mri_uq_2026-10-01/index.json) are archived. This is a full-text read of the preprint, not a reproduction or a check of the proceedings copy.

PHiRec is a conditional hierarchical VAE for de-aliasing undersampled multi-coil k-space measurements, trained on paired zero-filled and fully sampled reconstructions. Five latent resolutions generate 20 reconstruction samples per test input; a deterministic segmentation U-Net trained on fully sampled images maps each sample to a segmentation. The comparison includes MC dropout, a heteroscedastic head, their combination, a 20-network deep ensemble, a probabilistic U-Net and a non-UQ reconstruction U-Net for quality. All are evaluated on SKM-TEA knee raw k-space and six anatomical labels with official train/validation/test splits. In-domain models see 4x/8x/16x during training; the out-of-domain setting trains only at 4x and tests at 4x/8x/16x.

The authors evaluate reconstruction uncertainty by normalized cross-correlation (NCC) between sample variance and squared-error maps. For downstream segmentation, they compute per-pixel gamma maps from cross-entropy between each sampled segmentation and the mean, then compare gamma with segmentation-error maps using NCC. The text states PHiRec outperformed comparators in both measures and that its uncertainty increased consistently with acceleration. These results are displayed in Fig. 2 rather than a numeric text table; no exact effect size or confidence interval is inferred here. The authors explicitly note that their simple U-Net-based setup does **not enforce measured k-space data consistency**, and that acceleration shift may not induce a large enough epistemic shift for a clean separation.

**Collision decision.** The full text directly rules out broad novelty around propagating MRI reconstruction uncertainty to segmentation, using reconstruction disagreement to locate task error, or evaluating that relationship under acceleration shift. It starts from a fixed k-space input and samples a conditional reconstruction distribution; it does not hold a through-plane target/reference fixed while switching a companion between overlapping and disjoint *released neighboring slices*. Thus it is a close task-level precedent but not the same intervention as MIRA. MIRA still lacks task labels on the exact processed-DICOM targets and cannot claim Fischer's task-error correlation or a deployable safety score from retrospective `Delta O`. The separate [Kuestner 2024 access audit](2026-10-01_kuestner_fulltext_access_audit.md) remains unresolved; Fischer's coauthorship does not establish the later article's methods.
