I read the abstract, introduction, Methods 2.1--2.3, Experiments 3.1--3.3, conclusion and captions; rendered and inspected [p.3 method diagram](extracted/NPB_REC_2022_page3.png) and [p.8 Table 1/Figure 4](extracted/NPB_REC_2022_page8.png). References were scanned, not individually followed. The PDF says it was to appear at MLMIR 2022; that publication status was not independently verified, so this record cites the arXiv version.

**What is actually tested.** An E2E-VarNet receives a fixed retrospectively undersampled multi-coil fastMRI brain k-space input. During training, SGLD-like Gaussian gradient noise yields multiple saved weight states; at inference, nine predictions from the same backbone architecture and the same k-space input are averaged, and their pixelwise standard deviation is an uncertainty map (Fig. 1 and Eqs. 3--5). A matched nine-prediction Monte Carlo dropout baseline and a conventional E2E-VarNet are compared. The article reports 4,469 training, 113 validation and 247 inference images of 16 x 320 x 320 with 20 coils; 2,141 annotated slices are reported for evaluation. These are the paper's image/slice counts, **not independently verified patient counts**. It trains with random R=4 masks and evaluates random/equispaced R=4 and R=8; the inference set is drawn from fastMRI validation/fastMRI+ annotations rather than an independent clinical site.

For equispaced whole-image R=8, Table 1 reports PSNR/SSIM `34.55 +/- 5.01 / 0.908 +/- 0.09` for NPB-REC versus `33.08 +/- 4.82 / 0.897 +/- 0.092` for the conventional baseline and `32.25 +/- 4.74 / 0.891 +/- 0.09` for dropout. The abstract and Fig. 4 report Pearson `r=0.94` for mean image-standard-deviation versus reconstruction MSE, versus `r=0.91` for dropout. Fig. 4 plots the values on log axes. The main text does not establish external-site calibration, a clinical error threshold or a confidence guarantee; the correlation plot does not by itself identify per-pixel error. The paper's hyperparameter count of nine samples was selected on a 32-image subset of the inference set, which further limits any reading of Fig. 4 as a fully untouched test of all analysis choices.

**Collision decision.** MRI reconstruction uncertainty from multiple predictions, error correlation and a high-acceleration performance claim all precede MIRA. This paper's sampled outputs share the **same k-space input** and vary network weights; it does not fix one reconstruction/target and switch a companion interpolator between shared and disjoint **published physical through-plane slice sets**. Its Pearson correlation cannot be compared numerically with MIRA's patient-level `Delta O`, and its posterior-sampling result cannot support MIRA's deployment claim. The present MIRA difference remains a narrow, retrospective published-slice-context estimand rather than a first MRI uncertainty framework. This paper is one more reason to cite prior error/disagreement work explicitly; it does not resolve the missing Kuestner 2024 full text or prove global novelty.
