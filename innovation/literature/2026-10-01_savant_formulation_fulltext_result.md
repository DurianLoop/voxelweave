# Savant 2026: MRI slice interpolation formulation, full-text collision check

The [frozen targeted search](2026-10-01_mira_targeted_arxiv_refresh_plan.md) returned Savant, *Deep Learning for MRI Slice Interpolation: The Critical Role of Problem Formulation*, arXiv `2605.16476v1` (2026-05-15). The [21-page PDF](Savant_2026_MRI_Slice_Interpolation_Problem_Formulation.pdf) was retrieved from the fixed arXiv version, 14,307,161 bytes, SHA-256 `a7c6c90141ae8015a340b575417953d48a9c49a3fbfd23295d0d98850b023ce4`; see [source record](source_cache/savant_formulation_pdf_source.json) and [extracted text](extracted/Savant_2026_MRI_Slice_Interpolation_Problem_Formulation.txt). I read the main text, methods, Tables 1--3, dataset supplement and stated limitations; rendered p.5 to check the two result tables. I did not reproduce its models, inspect code, or verify the underlying DICOM selection. It is cited as an arXiv preprint; no peer-reviewed venue is inferred.

The paper uses T2 prostate MRI from **58 patients** in the TCIA UCLA biopsy collection and reports patient-separated training, validation and test subsets. A model predicts slice `i` from `i-k` and `i+k`. Its central comparison changes `k=2` (claimed 6 mm total span) to `k=1` (3 mm span), then retrains four deterministic architectures. Table 1 reports U-Net SSIM `0.57` to `0.898`; Table 2 reports U-Net `30.08 dB/0.8978` against linear `27.31 dB/0.8383` on 6,963 test samples. These are the author's reported slice-level numbers, not patient-equal results or performance replicated here. The appendix reports 46,329 slices from 58 volumes, about 799 per volume; the source does not reconcile that count with the stated 1.5 mm prostate through-plane protocol or explain whether slices include augmentations/multiple scans. This is a reproducibility question, not proof the results are wrong.

**Relevance and distinction.** The paper is a clear precedent that the chosen neighboring observations and physical distance can dominate architecture comparisons. It therefore constrains any broad MIRA sentence claiming to discover for the first time that MRI interpolation depends on its input context. Its `k=1` versus `k=2` comparison changes the *distance and difficulty* of the two-input interpolation task. MIRA instead holds one target, HAT reference, nominal four-slice interval, model weights and support fixed while the SwinIR companion uses either the same phase-0 released planes or phase-2 released planes; those phase sets have equal spacing but different physical positions and asymmetric target offsets, and the measured endpoint is paired-error `O`, not PSNR/SSIM improvement. Savant does not report this paired-error estimand or distinguish original acquired planes from processed release planes.

**Interpretation limits.** The manuscript's phrase that diffusion is fundamentally unsuited to deterministic interpolation is stronger than its comparison warrants: its conditional DDPM has 100 steps and 20 epochs versus 25 epochs for deterministic models, and no matched-compute or optimized diffusion comparison. Its "290x" impact ratio compares changes to input information with architecture changes, not matched-task effect sizes; Table 1's four `k=1` SSIM values span `0.892--0.898`, which is wider than a literal 0.2 percentage-point range. Do not reuse these claims as established facts. For MIRA, the useful lesson is to report every phase's physical positions and target offsets, keep equal-information controls, and avoid attributing a context effect solely to an architecture or native acquisition identity.
