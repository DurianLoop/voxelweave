# Segmentation-guided MRI reconstruction: full-text collision audit

Morshuis, Hein and Baumgartner, *Segmentation-guided MRI reconstruction for meaningfully diverse reconstructions*, arXiv:2407.18026v1 (2024), DOI `10.1007/978-3-031-72744-3_18`. The complete public [arXiv HTML](source_cache/segmentation_guided_mri_2026-10-01/article.html), [extracted text](source_cache/segmentation_guided_mri_2026-10-01/article.txt), source URL and SHA-256 are archived in the [retrieval index](source_cache/segmentation_guided_mri_2026-10-01/index.json). This is a methods/results-level read of the preprint, not an independent reproduction or a check of the later proceedings version.

The inverse problem is accelerated MRI from a fixed undersampled k-space observation, with Fourier/mask data consistency. At inference, segmentation-loss gradients guide the same diffusion reconstruction model toward two images per class: one increasing and one decreasing the predicted segmentation volume. The difference defines an "uncertainty boundary"; the comparator is repeated reconstruction sampling from the same measurement. This is a **different intervention** from MIRA's frozen target/reference and switched released through-plane companion observations.

The paper trains/tests on SKM-TEA knee E1 echoes. It evaluates every eighth axial slice in relevant regions, 869 slices for each of 4x, 8x, 12x and 16x acceleration. A U-Net trained on fully sampled images supplies four-class segmentation. At 16x, Table 1 reports lower-bound precision 0.980 and upper-bound recall 0.990 for guided reconstruction versus 0.951 and 0.957 for repeated reconstruction; uncertainty-volume ratio is 0.586 versus 0.113. Reconstruction SSIM is 0.788 versus 0.789, and PSNR 27.9 versus 28.0 dB. These are the authors' reported metrics; they do not establish calibrated coverage of all clinically plausible outcomes.

**Collision decision.** The paper rules out broad claims that MRI reconstruction diversity had not been connected to segmentation uncertainty, or that two deliberately different reconstructions plus a task network are new in themselves. It does not report MIRA's same-target released-neighbor-context switch, paired-error observability fraction, or brain T2 slice interpolation. The knee/k-space versus brain/through-plane distinction does not rescue a generic task-aware uncertainty novelty claim. A future UPENN tumor-mask extension would need a prespecified endpoint, exact atlas-space pairing, a same-observation baseline, and a comparison that separates context from the segmentation model; MIRA's present `Delta O` is not comparable to this paper's precision/recall.

The article cites Kuestner et al. 2024, but its reference is not a substitute for the still-unavailable Kuestner methods. The bounded non-overlap conclusion applies only to this read preprint.
