# Focused full-text check: MRI measurement dispersion and input sensitivity

This is a bounded novelty collision check, not a systematic review. The [queries](2026-10-01_shared_context_prior_art_search_plan.md) returned four unique arXiv IDs in [archived raw responses](search_snapshots/shared_context_2026-10-01/index.json). Two titles/abstracts warranted full-text review; the remaining two concern simultaneous multislice acquisition and quantitative mapping with complementary undersampling, not a same-target output-error audit. No paper was reproduced here. Publication status is reported as the retrieved arXiv version only.

## Lee and Choi, measured-subspace consistency (arXiv:2606.28448v1)

[Ten-page PDF](2026_Measured_Subspace_Consistency_MRI.pdf), SHA-256 `e91098888ee19098c26bd822494e06a24b1e34a1aa3dad4653738ebf0a34aa86`; [extracted text](extracted/2026_Measured_Subspace_Consistency_MRI.txt). Read the abstract, related work, full method/propositions, Tables 1--4, experimental setup, conclusion and limitations; rendered PDF pp.4 and 6 to verify equations (4)--(7), Table 2 and the image/dispersion figure. The source is an arXiv preprint dated 2026-06-26; no proceedings status is inferred.

The paper studies **multiple stochastic reconstructions conditioned on one fixed multi-coil undersampled k-space measurement**. It separates sample dispersion on already acquired coefficients (MSD) from unacquired coefficients (USD). A known forward operator and ideal pseudoinverse projection imply that pairwise post-projection sample differences lie in `N(A)`; the practical coil-combined lock has a residual cross-subspace coupling bound. It then wraps six diffusion posterior samplers with a terminal, training-free consistency correction. The main tests use fastMRI knee/brain, including a knee prior applied to brain and five brain contrasts; table cells are medians over 20 volumes x four central slices, usually eight chains and 300 reverse steps. Table 2's brain T2, R=8 DPS MSD falls from 59.1 to 3.9 in the table's `10^-4` units; PSNR changes from 23.4 to 23.5 dB. This is a measured-space physical-admissibility audit plus a corrective operator, not evidence about true MRI through-plane missing-slice error. The authors note measurement noise, practical coil coupling and non-Cartesian/3D/clinical generalization limits.

**Constraint on MIRA:** no broad claim to be the first black-box MRI measurement-dependence or disagreement audit. MIRA's present estimand is different: a deterministic fixed HAT prediction and companion prediction for the **same withheld published target plane**, with companion context switched between shared and disjoint released slices; `O` needs target truth and is retrospective. There is no known multi-coil forward operator, posterior sample distribution or guaranteed measured-subspace consistency in the current project. The two effects and Table 2 PSNR cannot be pooled or compared numerically.

## Bhutto et al., local Lipschitz reconstruction uncertainty (arXiv:2305.07618v3)

[Eleven-page PDF](2023_Local_Lipschitz_MRI_Uncertainty.pdf), SHA-256 `9b01b70c227785f9fb85f9e3c8158451af4c906e272062c2bc79187da9243783`; [extracted text](extracted/2023_Local_Lipschitz_MRI_Uncertainty.txt). Read the abstract, methodology, data contract, Tables/Figures 2--6, results and discussion; rendered PDF pp.7--8 for its input-perturbation pipeline, rank-correlation plots and threshold rule. Cite as arXiv v3 unless a formal venue is separately verified.

The paper calculates a local Lipschitz-like output response to injected k-space noise in AUTOMAP and relates that score to reconstruction MAE; it compares Monte-Carlo dropout and five-model ensembles and sketches a threshold-based alternative-reconstruction workflow. Its training uses 50,000 augmented HCP T1 brain 2D images; an in-distribution test uses 5,000 other brain images, and an out-of-distribution test uses 5,000 fastMRI knee images. Figure 4 shows in-distribution local-response/MAE rank correlations around 0.84--0.87 for the plotted settings; the text reports an out-of-distribution knee correlation of **0.4362** and identifies low-score/high-error false positives. Reported 99.94% AUC concerns **brain-versus-knee OOD detection** by response variance, not calibrated per-image correctness or MRI missing-slice risk. A threshold is illustrated on the in-distribution data; no current clinical deployment certificate follows.

**Constraint on MIRA:** input perturbation sensitivity, MRI error ranking and action thresholds already have direct prior work. The current MIRA phase-2 disjoint score also needs observations absent from phase-0 x4 sparse input, and its ReMIND target-ranking improvement interval crosses zero. It must remain a retrospective audit. A future acquired-only risk branch needs a separately sourced evaluation and fair comparison to a perturbation-response/local-Lipschitz score and supervised error predictors, with the exact perturbation and information budget matched. The paper's OOD failure reinforces why an in-source correlation or mean `Delta O` cannot be promoted to a deployment guarantee.

These two papers narrow language and baselines but do not directly duplicate MIRA's same-target published-slice context switch. The five arXiv queries are too narrow to certify global novelty; target-venue searches and a fresh full-text check remain necessary before submission.
