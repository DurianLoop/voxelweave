2.2. Learning Dynamic Sampling with Fixed Reconstructors via Deep RL
Heuristic sampling policies may not be adapted to specific datasets, which limits
their acceleration-quality trade-off. Since the MRI sampling process is carried out
over time and can be considered as a finite sequential decision-making process, a
dynamic sampling method was proposed to learn a sampler with a fixed pre-trained
reconstructor via deep RL. In Computed Tomography (CT) scanning, this method
was first proposed by [21], and a similar idea was later proposed in the MRI region.
These methods [11–13] formulate the MRI sampling trajectory as a Partially Observed
Markov Decision Process (POMDP) (see section 3.2) and learn parametric samplers
with a fixed pre-trained reconstructor by modern deep RL algorithms, such as DDQN
[22] and policy gradient [23]. RL enables the learning of a non-greedy sequential
decision function without relying on hand-designed policies. These methods use the
RL-learned policies to guide the scanning based on the measurements collected in the
preceding stage, thus achieving personalized scanning.
2.3. Jointly Learning Samplers and Reconstructors via Back-Propagation
If we fix a sampler to find an optimal reconstructor or fix a reconstructor to find
an optimal sampler, we may obtain suboptimal pairs of samplers and reconstructors.
Therefore, recent works aim to learn a good pair of samplers and reconstructors for
a better acceleration-quality trade-off. These works propose to jointly train samplers
and reconstructors end-to-end using Back-Propagation (BP).
One line of works [24–31] use a learnable column vector of probabilistic sub-
sampling masks p as the sampler, which is independent of the specific image, and
jointly train it together with a neural network-based reconstruction model R. These
works learn a better sampler p than human-designed heuristic sampling policies.
