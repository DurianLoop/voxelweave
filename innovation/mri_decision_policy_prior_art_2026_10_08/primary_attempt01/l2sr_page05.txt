3.1. Notation
In the context of this study, our focus is confined to the analysis of two-dimensional
(2D) accelerated MRI in conjunction with one-dimensional (1D) vertical Cartesian
sampling. We define a ground truth image, denoted by the matrix x∈ RN×N, and
the corresponding fully sampled measurements in the Fourier domain (referred to
as k-space in MRI data), represented by the matrix y∈ CN×N, obtained via the
two-dimensional Discrete Fourier Transform, F, i.e., y =F(x). For fully-observed
MRI scanning, the ground truth image can be reconstructed from the fully sampled
measurements by Inverse Fourier Transform, i.e., x =F−1(y).
Further, in accelerated MRI, we only observe partially sampled measurements.
Mathematically, the process of sampling is realized by masking y using a binary mask
matrix M∈{ 0, 1}N×N, rendering the observation of M⊙ y, where⊙ designates the
Hadamard product. In this study, all binary mask matrices satisfy the property that
each of their columns is either all 0’s or all 1’s, unless specified otherwise.
The process of reconstruction involves a specific mapping R : CN×N→ RN×N,
called the reconstructor, which maps (partially-observed) measurements to the
reconstructed image end-to-end. In this paper, it is customarily formulated as a
parametric neural network denoted byR(·;θR).
