4. Proposed Method
In this section, we first conduct an analysis of the dense-reward POMDP and identify
some inherent issues in section 4.1. Based on these insights, we then propose a novel
sparse-reward POMDP in section 4.2 to address the identified issues. Furthermore,
we present two novel training frameworks: dynamic sampling training framework in
section 4.3 and alternate training framework in section 4.4, both of which stem from
the sparse-reward POMDP.
4.1. The Issues with Dense-reward POMDP
Apart from the training mismatch issue arising from tackling the sub-optimization
problem, our comprehensive analysis of the dense-reward POMDP has revealed issues
associated with the MDP design. Specifically, the dense-reward POMDP embeds
reconstructions into the environment, particularly within transitions and rewards.
This design introduces two main problems: high computational cost and distributional
mismatch. These findings pave the way for the introduction of our sparse-reward
POMDP.
4.1.1. High Computational Cost Since the dense-reward POMDP involves
reconstructions in the transition, it first performs reconstruction (3.5) and then
sampling (3.6) at each step in the inference process. However, this approach is
very time-consuming since it requires performing many reconstructions. The dynamic
sampling method for CT scanning [21] uses the original sampling signals rather than
reconstructed images as the input of the sampler, which is more computationally
efficient. We aim to find a better POMDP that requires only one reconstruction in
the inference process.
