Acquisition: We define the acceleration factor as N/∥a∥1 to measure the time
overhead of sampling, denoted as ×(N/∥a∥1)-acceleration. Most sampling policies
start by acquiring a certain number of columns at the central low-frequency region,
where more information is concentrated, and then sample the rest of the k-space
according to their unique strategies. We define the initial acceleration factor as the
ratio between N and the number of sampling columns in the first state. We explore
two types of initialization: Base-horizon, where the initial acceleration factor is twice
the acceleration factor, and Long-horizon, where it is 8 times the acceleration factor.
Heuristic Sampling Policy for Pre-training : For the pre-training of
reconstructors Rdense and Rsparse, we employ a random sampling policy as the
heuristic approach. The specific acceleration factors and initial acceleration factors

grounded in a well-defined theoretical framework.
Dataset: We utilize the single-coil knee dataset and the multi-coil brain dataset
from the commonly-used fastMRI dataset [14]. The data preprocessing follows the
protocol established in [11]: We partition the dataset, reserving 20% of it for test
and the rest for training. For the single-coil knee dataset, we derive the ground truth
image x by cropping the original to the central 128×128 region, using half the available
volumes, and removing the outer slices of each volume, resulting in 6959 training slices,
1779 validation slices, and 1715 test slices. For the multi-coil brain dataset, we obtain
the ground truth image x by cropping to the central 256 × 256 region, using one-
fifth of the available volumes, and assembling a dataset of 11312 training slices, 4372
validation slices, and 2832 test slices.
Policy model: The policy model’s architecture is depicted in figure 3. It takes as
input the binary mask matrix Mt and the observation yt, and processes them through
a series of components including a 2D Inverse Fourier Transform, a feature extractor,
and actor and critic networks. The final outputs are a discrete probability distribution
π(·| yt;θπ) over actions, generated by the actor net, and a scalar value representing
the predicted value of the current state, produced by the critic net.
