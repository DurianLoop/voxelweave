5.2. Algorithm Comparisons
In the ‘Fixed Reconstructor’ setting, we evaluate the L2S against three baseline
methods: (1) Random: randomly selecting 1-d lines from a uniform distribution;
(2) PG-MRI [11]: solving the dense-reward POMDP by policy gradient (which is one
of the state-of-the-art dynamic sampling methods); (3) Greedy Oracle: a one-step
oracle policy that has access to ground truth at test time. We pre-train the dense-
reward reconstructorRdense with a mixture heuristic sampling policy for PG-MRI and
Greedy Oracle, and pre-train the sparse-reward reconstructor Rsparse with a terminal
heuristic sampling policy for Random and L2S.
In the ‘Joint Training’ setting, we evaluate the L2SR against two baseline
methods: (1) LOUPE [25]: jointly training a learnable non-sequential sampler and
a reconstructor; (2) τ-Step Seq. [35]: an advanced end-to-end sequential sampling and
reconstruction method, where τ indicates the number of sampling steps, typically set
to either 2 or 4 (and we report the best results obtained with either 2 or 4).
Additional details regarding all methods can be found in Appendix C.
5.3. Main Results
5.3.1. Reconstruction Performance The reconstruction results of all compared
accelerated MRI methods are quantified through SSIM and PSNR values, as detailed
in table 2, with more granular histograms presented in figure 4. The outcomes can be
understood in two key aspects. First, under the ’Fixed Reconstructor’ scenario, L2S
consistently outperforms other methods in most cases, signifying the importance of
addressing the distributional mismatch issue in enhancing reconstruction performance.
Secondly, L2SR emerges as the superior method in most cases across both settings.
This underlines the effectiveness of solving the overall joint optimization problem
compared to merely focusing on weakened forms or suboptimization issues.
5.3.2. Inference Complexity The computational complexities and corresponding
inference times of trained models are presented in table 3. Both L2S and L2SR
exhibit lower complexity and faster inference times compared to previous methods
such as PG-MRI and Greedy Oracle. This empirically substantiates our claim that
the proposed sparse-reward POMDP enhances efficiency by eliminating the need for
intermediate reconstructions.
While the utilization of sparse rewards may raise concerns regarding increased
training difficulty, the gains in inference efficiency make it a valuable tradeoff in
practical applications, especially in the field of medical imaging. The focus of our
experiments has been on assessing inference times, reflecting the emphasis on test
efficiency in real-world deployment scenarios.
