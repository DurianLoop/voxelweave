Every experiment in Section 3 tests a hypothesis. Nine
primary hypotheses, H1 to H9, each carry an explicit deci￾sion criterion (Table 9); eight secondary hypotheses pre￾dict specific effects that follow from the identities and
from the primary analyses (Appendix F); and where an
experiment has no decision criterion, its hypothesis is
marked exploratory and judged from intervals alone. A
hypothesis is called supported only if its criterion is met,
and a failed criterion is reported as such. The same 312
test subjects served both to develop the protocol and to
test these hypotheses, so the tests are exploratory, not
confirmatory: an explicit criterion prevents a hypothesis
from being bent to fit its outcome, but it cannot remove
the influence of other analyses of the same cohort. Confir￾mation requires an independent cohort, evaluated under
a pre-registered protocol in which the training set and
every threshold rule are fixed. All analyses run on stored
maps without a GPU; the cost of training and inference
is reported in Appendix D.10.
