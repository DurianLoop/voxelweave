3.2 Sensitivity of rankings to evaluation choices
Hypotheses. If evaluation choices matter, the order of two
methods should depend on them. H1 (ranking instability):
some pair of methods takes its minority order in at least
20% of defensible universes (lower 95% bound of ϕmax
at least 0.20). H2 (evaluation dominance): the evaluation
axes, with their interactions with method, explain more
of the variance of scores than the method does, with non￾overlapping intervals. Because H2 pools metrics that live
on different scales, we add an exploratory hypothesis that
sharpens it: a metric measures a property of the method
only if, within that metric, the method dominates the
variance and the order of methods does not flip.
Within each metric. The decomposition within a metric
separates rankings that belong to the method from rank￾ings that the evaluation decides (Table 3, Fig. 6). The
method explained 0.954 [0.933, 0.966] of the variance in
voxel AUROC and 0.960 [0.941, 0.972] in voxel AUPRC.
Voxel AUROC gave a single order in every universe (RE￾FLECT > cDDPM > UCCD > AnomalyDINO); in voxel
AUPRC only the aggregation changed the order, RE￾FLECT and cDDPM exchanging places between subject￾level and pooled values (Section 3.4). The method ex￾plained 0.770 [0.738, 0.787] of the variance in Dice, but
much of this is AnomalyDINO’s deficit. Amongst the
three residual models every pair swapped places in a sub￾stantial share of Dice universes (within-metric flip shares
0.36 to 0.49), Dice gave six different orders, and each of
REFLECT, UCCD and cDDPM ranked first in between
26% and 39% of them (Appendix C, Table 13). The bud￾get and the threshold protocol were the largest evaluation
axes for Dice (0.108 and 0.056 of its variance, main effect
plus interaction with method; point estimates). Lesion
sensitivity was governed by the evaluation: the method
explained 0.139 [0.127, 0.152] of its variance and the axes
0.657 [0.648, 0.666], of which the lesion definition carried
0.338 and the hit criterion 0.269 (point estimates). Part
of the lesion-definition share is a change of denominator
common to all methods (Section 3.7), which cannot re￾order them; the within-metric flip shares of 0.26 to 0.34
between residual models show that the evaluation choices
reorder them as well.
