Nominal budgets are not realised. At a nominal 20 cm3
the mean realised burden on test was 16.3 (REFLECT),
18.9 (UCCD), 12.9 (cDDPM) and 17.5 cm3
(Anomaly￾DINO). The median was 3.0 to 10.1 cm3
, the 95th per￾centile 37 to 64 cm3
, the maximum 210 to 604 cm3
, and
between 15.7% and 29.8% of subjects exceeded 20 cm3
(Table 4, Fig. 7a). The mean describes no typical subject;
it is pulled up by a minority with very large false-positive
volumes.
The transfer gap is a burden mismatch. The gap was 0.02
to 0.07 Dice for REFLECT and cDDPM at 5 to 20 cm3
,
because their validation thresholds were conservative on
test (REFLECT realised 3.3 cm3 at a nominal 5; cDDPM
12.9 cm3 at 20). For UCCD it was close to zero or negative
(−0.008 [−0.008, −0.007] at 5 cm3
): its validation thresh￾old almost equalled the test-matched one (0.385 against
0.381 at 20 cm3
) and was slightly liberal (5.3 cm3
realised
at 5). This is exactly the pattern Eq. (14) predicts: the
sign of g¯ follows the sign of b−βˆ, scaled by the local slope
of Ψ. Where Ψ is flat the gap vanishes even for a large mis￾match (UCCD at 40 cm3
: βˆ = 31.8, g¯ = −0.001). The gap
therefore measures how well a validation threshold trans￾fers, which differs between methods; it is not a uniform
test-set optimism. This is why the secondary hypothesis
of a positive UCCD gap at 5 cm3
failed, and why H6,
which required a lower bound of at least 0.02 at 20 cm3
,
narrowly failed (lower bounds 0.0191 for REFLECT and
0.0199 for cDDPM).
