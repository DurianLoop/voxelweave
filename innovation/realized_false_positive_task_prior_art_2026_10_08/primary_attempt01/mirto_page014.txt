3.4 Comparison of methods
Hypothesis (exploratory). With geometry gated and op￾erating points explicit, the residual models should still
differ in ways that hold across operating points for some
metrics but not others. Where pooled and subject-mean
Dice disagree, Identity 2 predicts that the method gaining
more from pooling is the one whose Dice is concentrated
in subjects with large pi +Gi
. The contrasts are tested in
declared families with Holm adjustment.
With the evaluation made explicit the residual mod￾els can be compared (Fig. 8; Appendix D.4, Table 33).
At small budgets UCCD and cDDPM achieve higher
Dice than REFLECT, also at equal burden: REFLECT
− UCCD was −0.200 [−0.219, −0.180] at 5 cm3 and
−0.118 [−0.209, −0.048] at equal burden (both pH <
0.005). At 20 cm3
the sign depended on the aggregation
(mean −0.042, median −0.008, pooled +0.020). At 40 cm3
pooled, REFLECT led by +0.056 [0.038, 0.073] at the
validation threshold and +0.060 at equal burden (both
pH < 0.005). Identity 2 gives the mechanism: pooling
raises a method’s Dice above its subject mean in pro￾portion to the covariance between Di and the weight
pi + Gi (Eq. 17). At 20 cm3 pooling lifted REFLECT
by 0.071 (0.497 → 0.568) but UCCD by only 0.009
(0.539 → 0.548), so REFLECT’s Dice is concentrated in
the subjects that carry the most weight. For a typical sub￾ject, as measured by the median, REFLECT and UCCD
were not distinguishable at 20 or 40 cm3 after adjustment
(pH from 0.14 to 1.00), whereas REFLECT − cDDPM at
40 cm3 was +0.055 [0.029, 0.079] in the median as well.
