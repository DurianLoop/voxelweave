3.5 Post-processing
Hypotheses. Identity 5 implies that a post-processing gain
measured at equal burden may come largely from relaxing
the plain threshold rather than from the post-processing
itself, and Identity 4 that deleting low-purity components
raises Dice whilst removing small true lesions. Secondary
hypotheses made this concrete: for UCCD at 20 cm3
the
relaxation term is positive, excludes zero and exceeds the
fixed-threshold term; for AnomalyDINO the total gain is
at most zero; and above-floor sensitivity falls with post￾processing for UCCD and AnomalyDINO. H5 (pruning
trade-off ): pruning helps lesion detection at 5 cm3 and
hurts it at 40 cm3
.
Hysteresis with persistence pruning raised Dice at
equal realised burden for the three residual models,
but almost all of the gain came from the burden it
freed (Table 7, Fig. 9). For UCCD at 20 cm3
the to￾tal of +0.085 split into +0.006 [−0.004, 0.016] from post￾processing at fixed threshold and +0.078 [0.073, 0.083]
from relaxing the plain threshold, as predicted: 92% of
the gain is relaxation. For REFLECT the step itself
lowered Dice (−0.013 [−0.022, −0.005]) and relaxation
supplied +0.046. For AnomalyDINO the total was null,
−0.001 [−0.011, 0.010], again as predicted. The step also
removed lesions: above-floor sensitivity fell from 0.674 to
0.629 (UCCD), 0.665 to 0.619 (cDDPM), 0.608 to 0.549
(REFLECT) and 0.210 to 0.121 (AnomalyDINO), and
small-lesion sensitivity fell to 0.000 for REFLECT. The
purity rule explains why: between 73.5% (AnomalyDINO)
and 82.5% (UCCD) of predicted components had purity
t
′/p′ < D/2, so deleting them raises Dice, and compo￾nents that contain small lesions are amongst them. H5
was not supported: the detection effect was negative at
both budgets for every method with Dice ≥ 0.3 (point
estimates only; no interval was computed for this mea￾sure). The three secondary hypotheses held. Results at
every budget are in Appendix D.5.
