The threshold-transfer component turns a familiar
complaint, that thresholds are tuned on the test set, into
something measurable. Identity 1 shows that the gap be￾tween test-tuned and validation-tuned Dice is not a fixed
optimism to be subtracted but a product of a burden
mismatch and a local slope, which differs from method to
method. A comparison at validation thresholds reflects
what a user would obtain but confounds map quality
with threshold transfer; a comparison at equal burden
isolates map quality but cannot be reproduced in prac￾tice. MIRTO reports both, and the identity says exactly
how they relate. Conformal risk control adds a per-scan
guarantee, which is closer to what matters for an indi￾vidual patient than a cohort mean. The known training￾conditional law of Lemma 1 adds the caveat that a 24-
subject calibration set honours the guarantee only on av￾erage, with a spread that follows from the calibration size
alone, and the empirical splits followed that law closely
(shares within 2.8 percentage points, 97.5th percentiles
within 0.015). A user who needs the guarantee for the
calibration set in hand should use RCPS or Learn-then￾Test [8,2] and accept a stricter threshold.
The multiverse quantifies what the previous compo￾nents illustrate, and it is most informative metric by met￾ric. Pooled over metrics, the evaluation axes carry most
of the variance, but mainly because metrics have differ￾ent scales, and we do not lean on that share. Within a
metric the picture is sharper. Rankings by voxel AUROC
are a property of the method. Dice is dominated by the
method in variance, chiefly because one method is far be￾hind, yet the order of the three residual models is decided
by the evaluation, with leads of several Dice points in each
direction and significant contrasts on both sides. Lesion
sensitivity is mostly a property of the evaluation. Read￾ing flip shares together with the size of each difference is
what separates these cases from mere closeness: UCCD
and cDDPM swap places often because they are close,
and their significant difference at validation thresholds is
a matter of threshold transfer. Lesion-level claims deserve
the least confidence unless the lesion definition, the hit
criterion and the floor are stated, and our denominator
identity shows that the floor alone can change sensitivity
elevenfold without a single hit changing.
Recommendations. For authors of UAD methods we sug￾gest a short list, which the checklist released with the code
expands. Score every method against one reference on one
grid, and report a registration check. Choose thresholds
on data that are not the test set, and report the realised
false-positive burden as a distribution (median and 95th
percentile, not only the mean). Report subject mean, me￾dian and pooled Dice side by side, and when two meth￾ods swap places across evaluation choices, report by how
much each leads. State the lesion definition in full. Do not
report marginal slice AUROC without an anatomy con￾trol. Report paired intervals with a declared multiplicity
correction. And train competing methods on the same
healthy data, or say plainly that they were not.
Relation to other benchmarks. Recent benchmarks have
improved UAD evaluation by enlarging and diversifying
the test data [28], by examining the normative recon￾struction [16], or by unifying many methods and datasets
within one framework [40,6,18]. MIRTO is complemen￾tary. It does not propose a new dataset; it asks how much
of any benchmark’s conclusion survives a change of de￾fensible evaluation choices, and it provides the identities
needed to say why a conclusion changes when it does. The
same machinery could be wrapped around any of those
benchmarks. It also answers, for UAD, the concerns raised
