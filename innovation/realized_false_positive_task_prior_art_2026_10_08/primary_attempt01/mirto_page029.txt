by up to 0.046 relative to mid-ranks; MIRTO uses mid-ranks and tie-collapsed average precision throughout.

Spatial randomisation controls (Table 23) apply known displacements to the stored maps: through-plane shifts by
±2, ±5 and ±10 slices, a permutation of slices within the subject, and the exchange of maps across subjects matched
on brain area. Shifting a map by two slices costs at most 0.009 Dice, by ten slices 0.08 to 0.12 for the residual
models; permuting slices within a subject or exchanging maps across subjects removes most of the Dice, leaving
0.06 to 0.07 when maps come from another subject. This confirms that Dice at the validation threshold depends on
subject-specific localisation, as it should.
