Whereas in clinical and medical physics the image acquisition method de￾fines the imaging modality (e.g., computerised tomography, MRI, positron￾emission tomography), in the field of computer science, it is becoming more
common to refer to each distinct sequence type, even within MRI, as a modal￾ity. In this manuscript we have adopted this definition, for compatibility with
current literature in the field of medical image segmentation.
A standard multi-modal segmentation model requires a fixed set of input
modalities. A hetero-modal model, however, is designed to generate predic￾tions from any available subset of imaging modalities. The seminal work in
this area is HeMIS (Hetero-modal Medical Image Segmentation) by Havaei
et al. (2016), which proposed the use of independent encoders to embed each
modality into a shared latent space. Subsequent work (Dorent et al., 2019)
incorporated variational autoencoders to reconstruct the missing modalities.
More recently, convolutional neural networks (LeCun et al., 1989) have been
combined with vision transformers (Vaswani et al., 2017) for more flexible
hetero-modal processing (Zhang et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2025;
Lan et al., 2025).
Much of the literature focuses on hetero-modal inference, but hetero￾modal learning is arguably the more important aspect of the framework.
The primary advantage of training on a dataset where not all samples have
a complete set of modalities is the ability to include a larger number of in￾dividual subjects during training. This allows a model to capture a greater
proportion of the anatomical and pathological variation present in the un￾derlying clinical population. In theory, this also allows models to leverage
“privileged information”—meaning that learning from combined modalities
can ultimately improve the model’s single-modality performance by encour￾aging richer, correlated feature representations. In practice, however, this
depends heavily on the dataset and the specific clinical task, and modality￾specialist baselines still frequently outperform hetero-modal methods when
trained on the same set of subjects.
