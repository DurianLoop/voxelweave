The recent literature has begun to explore hetero-modal learning. Shi
et al. (2024) simulated imbalanced, incomplete modality scenarios in the
BraTS (Menze et al., 2014) and MyOPS (Li et al., 2023) datasets. Lan
et al. (2025) studied incomplete hetero-modal learning under varying miss￾ing rates using BraTS. Wagner et al. (2025) investigated federated learning in
seven datasets comprising seven MRI sequences and five pathologies. Using a
multi-channel U-Net (Ronneberger et al., 2015) with zero-imputation (pass￾ing missing modalities into the network as channels of zeros), they showed
their model could perform segmentation on unseen modality combinations.
In particular, they used the WMH segmentation challenge dataset (Kuijf
et al., 2019) for WMH segmentation (T1w and FLAIR), alongside the AT￾LAS v2.0 (Liew et al., 2022) (T1w only) and ISLES (Hernandez Petzsche
et al., 2022) (T1w, T2w, FLAIR, and DWI) datasets for ISL segmentation.
However, all pathologies were merged into a single “lesion” class. While the
WMH Challenge, ATLAS v2.0, and ISLES datasets contain ground truth
segmentations for WMH or ISL independently, no publicly available dataset
currently provides joint ground truth annotations for both pathologies. This
lack of joint labels restricts a model’s ability to accurately penalise false
negatives when these visually similar lesions co-occur. Furthermore, mild
SVD-related ISL (i.e., lacunar strokes) are under-represented in the ATLAS
and ISLES cohorts. To address this gap, we leverage a privately held dataset
containing expertly annotated ground truth segmentations for both WMH
and ISL, with four distinct MRI sequences available (T1w, T2w, FLAIR, and
DWI).
While utilising this unique dataset allows us to rigorously evaluate hetero￾modal learning for joint segmentation of WMH and ISL, real-world clinical
deployment presents an additional hurdle: silent data corruption. A lim￾itation of current hetero-modal approaches is the assumption that missing
modalities are always correctly identified by the system. In automated im￾age processing workflows, this is frequently not the case. MRI files might
be filled with zeros or other constant voxel values, or a scan might be cor￾rupted by artefacts severe enough to make it unusable. To overcome this,
we introduce an additional layer of extreme data augmentation which we
refer to as “corruption augmentation”. With this corruption augmentation in
mind, we develop the Multimodal Attention Router (MMAR) block. While
we do not comprehensively benchmark our MMAR-based model against the
entirety of the hetero-modal literature, we present it as a proof-of-concept
