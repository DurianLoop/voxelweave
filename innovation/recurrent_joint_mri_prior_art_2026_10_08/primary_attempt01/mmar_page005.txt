This paper makes the following contributions:
1. We establish the first benchmark for joint hetero-modal segmentation
of co-occurring WMH and ISL by leveraging a dataset of patients with
subacute and chronic ISL and various degrees of vascular pathology,
with ground truth annotations for both pathologies, and four MRI
sequences.
2. We show that hetero-modal learning can train significantly better mod￾els than fixed-modality baselines under a training data distribution
containing only a small (10%) full-modality subset, with the remainder
being strictly uni-modal.
3. We demonstrate that hetero-modal learning can produce models capa￾ble of performing inference on unseen combinations of modalities.
4. To address the vulnerability of hetero-modal networks to silent data
degradation, we introduce the Multimodal Attention Router (MMAR)
block, an architectural solution designed to for dynamic feature routing,
ensuring robustness against corrupted inputs.
5. We show that when trained with a “corruption augmentation” strategy,
our MMAR-based model maintains strong performance in the face of
catastrophic noise, severe bias field corruption, and modalities incor￾rectly identified as being present.
2. Experimental setup
2.1. Dataset and processing
We performed our experiments on the Mild Stroke Study 3 dataset (Clancy
et al., 2021)—a prospective observational cohort study of patients presenting
with clinically evident stroke syndromes, which was granted ethical approval
by Southeast Scotland Regional Ethics Committee (ref. 18/SS/0044). T1w
(3D protocol, 1.0 × 1.0 × 1.0 mm voxels), T2w (3D protocol, 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9
mm voxels), FLAIR (3D protocol, 1.0 × 1.0 × 1.0 mm voxels), and DWI (2D
protocol, 2.0×2.0×2.0 mm voxels) MRI scans were acquired using a 3 Tesla
Siemens Prisma MRI scanner. An expert manually delineated ISL masks,
