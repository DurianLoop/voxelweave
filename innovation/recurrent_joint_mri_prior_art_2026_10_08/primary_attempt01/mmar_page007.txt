2.2.2. Training split 2
In this highly uni-modal setup, a complete set of sequences is available
for only a small subset (10%) of the data, while the remaining subjects
contribute only a single sequence. We designed this split to test whether
hetero-modal learning can improve predictive performance on specific target
sequences by leveraging additional, disjointed training data that a standard
specialist model would otherwise be unable to access.
2.2.3. Training split 3
In this setup, we designated T1w as the “anchor sequence” due to its
high prevalence in standard MRI protocols. All other sequences overlap with
this T1w anchor, but never with each other. We used this distribution to
investigate whether hetero-modal learning can train models capable of per￾forming inference on novel, unseen combinations of sequences (e.g., FLAIR
and DWI).
