2.3. Checkpoint selection
Because our primary objective is to evaluate the benefits of hetero-modal
learning (i.e., maximising training data) rather than hetero-modal inference
(i.e., using a single set of weights for all possible inputs), we evaluated the
model using the checkpoint that achieved the highest validation Dice Simi￾larity Coefficient (DSC) for each specific inference modality setup. In other
words, for each of the hetero-modal learning methods, we store 15 different
model checkpoints from a single training run. This ensures the fairest pos￾sible comparison against the modality specialists, which naturally use their
own optimal DSC checkpoints.
However, for the corruption augmentation experiments, we assume that
corrupted modalities cannot be identified a priori (otherwise, they would
simply be treated as missing). Consequently, for those specific tests, we used
the final model checkpoints—representing the state where the models have
converged to a stable local minimum as the learning rate decays to zero.
3. Methods
We implemented two established baseline methods for hetero-modal seg￾mentation: a multi-channel U-Net (Ronneberger et al., 2015) and U-HeMIS
(Havaei et al., 2016). We also developed and evaluated our own archi￾tecture, the Multimodal Attention Router (MMAR), designed to enhance
model robustness against corrupted modalities. We compared these hetero￾modal methods to modality specialist baselines. This section details each
of these architectures and our training methodologies. Code is available at:
https://github.com/Jesse-Phitidis/MultiModal.
3.1. Modality specialist baselines
We trained modality-specialist baselines to serve as comparative bench￾marks for the hetero-modal approaches. Each specialist model is trained and
evaluated on a fixed, unique, modality combination. Consequently, these
models cannot leverage the full multi-modal training data present in splits
2 and 3 (with the exception of the T1w specialist in training split 3). To
prevent overfitting on their simpler learning tasks, the specialist baselines
were configured with a standard 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, and 320 channels per
resolution level, totalling approximately 16.7M parameters.
