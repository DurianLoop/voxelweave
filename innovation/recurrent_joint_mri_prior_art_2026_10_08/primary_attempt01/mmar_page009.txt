3.2. Multi-channel U-Net
A multi-channel U-Net serves as the most straightforward hetero-modal
method. Missing modalities are handled via zero-imputation (passed as chan￾nels of zeros), requiring the network to handle extreme distribution shifts
within its very first layers. To ensure a fair comparison and provide the
model with sufficient capacity to learn robustness to missing channels, we
matched the parameter counts of the other hetero-modal methods (91M pa￾rameters) by configuring a large U-Net with 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, and 1024
channels at each respective resolution level.
3.3. U-HeMIS
As introduced in Section 1, HeMIS utilises an independent convolutional
encoder for each modality. U-HeMIS extends this logic to the skip connec￾tions at every level of a U-Net architecture. At each resolution level, the
network calculates the mean and variance across the feature maps extracted
from the available modalities. These statistical representations are then con￾catenated along the channel dimension and processed by a single, shared
convolutional decoder. For our experiments, we configured each independent
encoder with 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, and 512 channels.
3.4. Multimodal Attention Router (MMAR)
Motivated by the challenge presented by corrupted modalities which are
not marked as missing, we propose the Multimodal Attention Router (MMAR)
block. Our method, shown in Figure 3, is inspired by U-HeMIS and SFusion
(Liu et al., 2023).
3.4.1. Overview
Our MMAR-based model replaces the statistical feature aggregation of U￾HeMIS with a learnable dynamic feature router over the spatial and channel
dimensions. The output is a single fused feature representation, with the
important channels and spatial locations of each modality amplified, while
irrelevant features are suppressed. This allows the MMAR to learn to down￾weight features generated from corrupted modalities.
