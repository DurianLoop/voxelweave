3.5.4. Corruption augmentation
To equip the hetero-modal models with resilience to unidentified cor￾rupted modalities, we performed some of our experiments with an additional
layer of extreme data augmentation severe enough to render a scan almost or
completely unusable. During training for these experiments, we reassigned
(with 50% probability per scan) some of the D scans that were initially se￾lected to undergo modality dropout, to undergo corruption instead. For each
corrupted scan, the corruption transform was randomly selected as one of the
following: gaussian noise (std=3); bias field (polynomial degree 3, coefficients
in [0.4, 0.9]); low contrast (multiplier in [0, 0.1]); high contrast (multiplier
in [10, 30]); Gaussian blur (sigma in [2, 6] per axis); Gibbs noise (alpha in
[0.75, 1]); K-Space spike (intensity in [15, 18]); Rician noise (std=1). We
randomly performed each of these corruptions on the entire scan, or on ran￾dom patches of the scan (3-10 patches, each of size 40-80 voxels in each axis).
We implemented these transformations using the MONAI library (Cardoso
et al., 2022). Figure 4 shows examples.
