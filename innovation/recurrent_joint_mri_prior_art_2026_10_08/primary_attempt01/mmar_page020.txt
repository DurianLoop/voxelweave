4.3. Hetero-modal models perform well on unseen modality combinations
Table 3 outlines the results for training split 3, further reinforcing the
benefits of hetero-modal learning. The specialist models only achieved the
best result during T1w inference—the single “anchor” modality present across
all training samples. Conversely, the MMAR architecture demonstrated the
strongest performance on 6 out of 8 novel modality combinations. Its mean
macro-average score was significantly higher than that of U-HeMIS, though
not significantly better than the multi-channel U-Net. We attribute this
success to the MMAR’s locally adaptive, dynamic feature weighting, which
handles novel modality combinations more robustly than the fixed statistical
merging used in U-HeMIS.
