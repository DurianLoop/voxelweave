4.4. MMAR outperforms established hetero-modal models on corrupted data
The final experiments evaluated model robustness against severe data
degradation. We applied corruption augmentation during training on split
1 and tested under four scenarios: completely missing modalities, “false
present” modalities (missing but unidentified as such), catastrophic Gaus￾sian noise, and severe bias fields.
As shown in Table 4, when predicting with explicitly missing modal￾ities, the multi-channel U-Net and MMAR performed slightly worse than
they did without corruption augmentation, whereas U-HeMIS showed slight
improvement.
However, the results shift under the remaining three corruption scenarios
(Tables 5, 6, and 7). Faced with false presents, Gaussian noise, or severe
bias fields, the MMAR consistently outperformed both the multi-channel
U-Net and U-HeMIS for both WMH and ISL segmentation, and exhibited
minimal performance degradation when transitioning from explicitly missing
to corrupted modality testing.
We attribute the MMAR’s success to its router-based architecture and
auxiliary router loss. During training, we observed the router loss drop
rapidly and remain low. This mechanism drastically reduces the gradient
flow to any encoder processing a corrupted image, protecting that encoder
from wasting learning capacity on becoming robust to catastrophic noise.
