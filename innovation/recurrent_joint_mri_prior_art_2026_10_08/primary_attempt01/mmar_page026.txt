The first row illustrates the baseline output when all modalities are clean
and present. Here, the router is highly selective about where it assigns high
attention weights to the DWI scan. The two prominent bright areas strongly
align with the locations of the ISL, while weights remain comparatively sup￾pressed outside these regions. This aligns with clinical intuition: DWI is a
powerful modality for identifying acute ISL (often termed “DWI-positive”)
but generally lacks anatomical detail and is acquired at a lower resolution.
When FLAIR is completely missing, the attention distributions shift
significantly—most notably for T2w and DWI. Features previously domi￾nated by the FLAIR sequence in the fused representation are drawn from the
remaining available modalities. Specifically, the network relies more heavily
on the DWI encoder for grey matter features and the T2w encoder for back￾ground features.
When the FLAIR scan is subjected to catastrophic corruption (Gaussian
noise, false present), the router assigns it negligible weight. The resulting
attention maps for T1w, T2w, and DWI closely resemble those of the explic￾itly missing FLAIR scenario. This demonstrates that the MMAR naturally
learns to treat severely corrupted modalities as if they were missing. Under
bias field corruption, the router leverages its spatial adaptivity to selectively
allow FLAIR features to pass into the hetero-modal fusion only in regions
where the scan remains less affected by the artefact.
Figure 7 plots the mean router weights on the validation set at each en￾coder level during full-modality inference (using the final model checkpoint),
comparing models trained across the three different data splits (without cor￾ruption augmentation). Across training splits 1 and 2, FLAIR dominates the
three highest resolution levels (levels 0-2).
However, these plots reveal that the underlying training data distribution
influences the weighting strategy learned by the router. For instance, in
training split 3—where T1w scans are the “anchor” modality and far more
abundant than the others—the router learns to prioritise T1w features. Here,
FLAIR only retains its dominance at the absolute highest resolution level.
This result is a logical consequence of the network maximising the utility of
the T1w encoder, which naturally becomes a stronger feature extractor due
to the higher T1w representation in the training data.
