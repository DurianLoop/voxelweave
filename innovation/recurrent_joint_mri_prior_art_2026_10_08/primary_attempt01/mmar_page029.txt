5. Conclusion
This study establishes the first benchmark for the joint hetero-modal
segmentation of co-occurring white matter hyperintensities (WMH) and is￾chaemic stroke lesions (ISL). By leveraging a unique, fully annotated, multi￾sequence dataset, we demonstrated that hetero-modal learning effectively
overcomes the rigid data requirements of traditional modality specialist mod￾els. Rather than discarding incomplete scans, this approach maximises the
utility of all available data. We showed this to be effective in data-constrained
scenarios, with hetero-modal models generally outperforming modality spe￾cialist baselines.
Beyond accommodating missing data during training, our findings high￾light the adaptability of hetero-modal networks at inference. When trained
on disjointed datasets sharing only a single anchor modality, these models
successfully generalised to novel modality combinations, maintaining robust
predictive performance even when the highly informative FLAIR sequence
was unavailable.
Additionally, we address a major hurdle in clinical deployment: silent data
degradation. By introducing the Multimodal Attention Router (MMAR)
block alongside a complimentary corruption augmentation strategy, we showed
that this proof-of-concept method can dynamically adapt to unflagged, catas￾trophically corrupted modalities. Through spatially adaptive feature routing,
the MMAR-based model learns to treat severely degraded regions in scans as
missing, preserving segmentation accuracy in the presence of extreme noise,
bias fields, and false-present modalities.
Ultimately, these strategies offer a robust and practical pathway for de￾veloping and deploying medical image segmentation tools in imperfect, real￾world clinical environments.

likely that our comparative results are highly sensitive to the total volume
and modality distribution of the training data; we urge caution when extrap￾olating our results to other data distributions. Second, due to the number of

evaluations are centred on a single random training, validation, and test split,
and each model was trained only once. While we attempted to mitigate the
