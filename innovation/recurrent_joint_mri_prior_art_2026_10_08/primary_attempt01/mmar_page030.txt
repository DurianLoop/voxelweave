standard segmentation metric and correlates strongly with other measures.
Since our primary objective was the relative comparison of methods rather
than establishing absolute task performance, limiting our evaluation to DSC
allowed us to reduce the complexity of the results while preserving statis￾tical power. Fourth, due to a lack of truly corrupted scans in our dataset,
test time corruption transformations were required for evaluating the models
that were trained for corruption-robustness, using corruption augmentation.
To mitigate this bias, we applied different transformation parameters during
training and testing augmentations. Finally, because our primary motivation
was to evaluate the viability of hetero-modal learning in data-starved clini￾cal scenarios, we opted for foundational, proven baseline methods. However,
an abundance of complex architectures and training techniques have been
proposed for imbalanced hetero-modal learning—typically evaluated on the
BraTS brain tumour dataset (Menze et al., 2014). We leave a systematic
benchmarking of these methods applied to WMH and ISL segmentation for
future work.
